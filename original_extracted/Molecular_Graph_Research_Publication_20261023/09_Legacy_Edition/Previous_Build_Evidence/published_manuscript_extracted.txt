Abstract
Background. The capacity of a temporally evolving spiking network to retain decodable information about
graph organization can be investigated through controlled contact interventions.
Methods. We analyze original compiled spiking-network records spanning a six-case paired graph-classification
study, four additional RNA-labelled negative tests, eight chemical-bond graph ablations, five RNA input sequences
and 13 molecular-mechanics structures. Matched graph contrasts used deterministic seed resets, controlled
stimulation, feedback and plasticity manipulations. Complete 48-tick target-neuron masks were independently
rechecked for 13,312 paired comparisons.
Results. In the 1KF1-labelled computational graph, the classifier reached 196/256 correct (76.56%) under a cycle-
breaking rewire, but 116/256 (45.31%) under a different ring-preserving rewire. The chemical and contact-derived
runs yielded nonzero response differences in their supported conditions; they also contained null and reversed
learning effects. Thirteen MMFF94 minimizations reported convergence with sampled analytical/numerical
gradient agreement. The 13,312 mask-derived disagreement records matched the archived counts.
Interpretation. The compiled network exhibits reproducible sensitivity to selected computational contact
interventions. These data do not establish general structural equivalence recognition, experimental RNA structure
prediction quality or ligand affinity.
Keywords: spiking neural network; directed graph cycles; molecular representations; contact ablation; deter-
ministic replay; RNA secondary structure; molecular mechanics.


1     Introduction
Changes in contact organization can affect a dynamical neural system even when other input properties are
held fixed. To investigate this question without equating computational trajectories with physical chemistry, the
experiments considered graph structures associated with deposited DNA and RNA sequences, SMILES-encoded
molecular connectivity, and subsequently computed RNA pairing and nonbonded spatial contacts.
The central objective is to quantify which computational interventions produced decodable or changed neural
activity, not to infer the unknown three-dimensional fold of a molecule. Two endpoints are analyzed separately:
held-out classification of intervention identity in the original graph experiments, and the fraction of changed
target-neuron firing positions in later paired mask experiments. These endpoints use different experimental
units, denominators and hypotheses and are not interchangeable.
Existing molecular folding tools such as ViennaRNA RNAfold predict minimum-free-energy secondary structures
and related ensemble observables. Published spiking graph-learning methods and classical graph algorithms also
offer distinct comparison targets. No matched benchmark of this particular experiment against those methods
appears in the preserved records. Direct cross-paper superiority claims are therefore inappropriate [1, 4].


2     Hypotheses and scope
H1 (graph perturbation): Changes to selected constructed contact graphs can be discriminated from native
spiking activity under held-out deterministic seeds.
H2 (cycle selectivity): A cycle-breaking graph contrast and a cycle-preserving contrast need not produce
equal classification performance.
H3 (contact influence): Removing chemical-bond, RNA-pairing, or force-derived contacts can change the
native SNN spike masks for matched computational inputs.
Not tested: Prediction of measured molecular binding, held-out physical folding coordinates, chemical reaction
rates, or medical outcomes.


3     Materials and methods
3.1    Native network and experimental unit
The preserved original computational scaffold contains 118 neurons and 324 directed synapses. Experiment-
specific input nodes and contact edges are added without replacing the compiled native state-transition routine.
Runs consist of 48 deterministic ticks per independently reset network. A paired comparison holds seed and

declared exogenous stimulation policy matched while varying the recorded contact graph. A seed reset is the
unit of computational variation; it is not a biological replicate.
The experimental factors cross native-output-conditioned feedback versus a matched-current fixed schedule
with synaptic learning enabled (spike-timing-dependent plasticity, STDP) versus frozen connection weights.
The feedback-selected input target can depend on prior native spike output and evolved weights; it is not a
chemistry-aware autonomous scientist.

the alternative computational contact graphs were manually constructed rather than derived from complete

classifier evaluated 256 graph labels over 128 fresh stimulus seed pairs. Original classifier readouts use late

3.3    Constructed cycle controls
For the telomeric quadruplex-labelled graph, the original constructed contacts contain directed four-site rings.
The coarse rewire replaces their offset-1 arrows with offset-2 arrows, splitting a four-cycle into two reciprocal
two-cycles while preserving directed vertex degrees. The subsequently introduced offset-3 alternative preserves
each four-cycle while changing its orientation. This was a post hoc control, not prospective independent physical
confirmation. Figure 3 draws only the source-described graph construction.

3.5    Chemical-bond and RNA-contact perturbations
Chemical structures in the source are eight RDKit-validated SMILES compounds. The bond-graph experiment
retains the same atoms while removing the declared bond-derived computational edges. Later input generation
uses RDKit ETKDG conformers and MMFF94 optimization to derive spatial contacts, while RNA input
generation uses weighted noncrossing base-pair optimization and, in a subsequent branch, selected Turner-derived
stack/hairpin terms. The later branch also searches for crossing H-type stems using a separate heuristic scoring
criterion. These model outputs should not be called a complete RNA folding free-energy landscape.


3.6    Atomistic force evaluation
RDKit MMFF94 was used for eight small-molecule structures and five four-nucleotide RNA fragments. Sampled
Cartesian analytical derivatives were compared with centered finite differences, with four checked components
per molecular entry in the final record. A small sampled gradient discrepancy confirms consistency of those
calculations; it does not establish experimentally correct forces or full-RNA fold geometry [2].

3.7    Data analysis and independent checks
The archived result table contains 13,312 paired records: 2,048 bond-graph comparisons, 3,328 predicted-contact
comparisons and 7,936 extended model/force-contact comparisons. The underlying 48-tick mask pairs were
independently audited and grouped into 208 condition summaries. A separate table retains 100 historical
graph-classifier result rows. The original logs list 81 passing Python test cases for the final pipeline. The latter
are historical executions, not tests newly executed by this manuscript-compilation step.


4     Results
4.1    Held-out discrimination and cycle controls
The constructed 1KF1 cycle-breaking comparison achieved 196/256 correct predictions (76.56%) on fresh held-out
seeds, with a six-case Holm-adjusted probability of 0.02783. A separate ring-preserving comparison produced
116/256 correct (45.31%) and did not demonstrate reliable separation under that readout. This pattern is
consistent with a sensitivity to the changed coarse cycle organization. Chance-level performance in the second
comparison does not prove generalized graph-equivalence recognition. The remaining six-case values and negative
results appear in Table 1.

4.2    Four additional RNA-labelled contact graph cases
The four later RNA-labelled cases did not pass their documented multiple-test correction. Their measured
primary accuracies were 44.92% for 1ZIF, 44.92% for 1ZIG, 53.52% for 2KOC and 48.05% for 1EHZ. These are
classification of constructed graph interventions, not four external RNA folding tests.


4.3    Chemical-bond graph ablations
Eight chemically valid SMILES inputs were screened under the two feedback modes and two plasticity states.
The result was the proportion of target-neuron firing positions that changed when bond-derived contact edges
were removed. For caffeine, the native-feedback/STDP condition yielded 17.17% altered firing positions;
the corresponding frozen-weight condition yielded 10.93%. Such differences demonstrate sensitivity to the
computational intervention, not stimulant activity, toxicity or affinity. The full factorial values are shown in
Figure 5 and the per-seed caffeine distribution is retained rather than reporting only means.


4.4    RNA structure calculations and contact ablations
For the five RNA input sequences, the earlier weighted nested-pair solver predicted respectively 4, 4, 4, 5 and 26
pairs; the selected-energy-term model predicted 4, 4, 4, 5 and 23 pairs. The later model outputs were −2.74,
−2.74, −2.74, −5.43 and −14.60356 kcal/mol. They reflect its implemented model terms, including simplified
internal/bulge penalties, and are not experimentally measured RNA free energies. A separate crossing-stem
search selected a 1EHZ candidate; the candidate’s heuristic score is not Gibbs free energy and does not validate
the actual tRNA tertiary fold.

4.5    MMFF94 molecular mechanics and spatial contacts
Eight small molecules and five four-nucleotide RNA fragments were subjected to RDKit MMFF94 minimization.
The final record reports convergence for all 13 cases and a largest sampled analytical-vs-numerical gradient
difference of 1.59 × 10−7 kcal/mol/Å. The five RNA inputs are short fragments, not whole RNA atomistic folding
predictions. The fragments attached to 1ZIH, 1ZIF and 1ZIG share their first four nucleotides, and therefore
share computed atomic outputs; they are not independent molecular confirmations. The coordinate-derived
contacts and the recorded neural-response differences depend on the implemented contact definition.


4.6    Recorded execution and regression
The final molecular analysis campaign produced 7,936 paired comparisons, representing 15,872 network resets at
48 ticks each, or 761,856 SNN ticks. One recorded end-to-end pipeline run took 32.067 seconds on its execution
host, excluding a fresh full upstream rebuild. This is a single-host measured workload, not a task-matched speed
comparison with independent molecular simulators. Cross-compiler/sanitizer evidence and 81 named historical
Python test outcomes are preserved in the supplementary logs. The later one-shot upstream test command was
interrupted, although individual follow-up commands passed; it is not described as a full one-shot successful
rerun.


5     Discussion
The principal computational observation is that a stateful native spiking network can exhibit reproducible
contact-graph sensitivity under specified interventions. The telomeric quadruplex-labelled cycle experiment offers
a particularly interpretable result because its non-significant ring-preserving control narrows the mechanism
consistent with the original contrast. The later chemical, pairing-derived and post-force contact experiments
demonstrate additional input pathways into the same native network, but use different interventions and should
not be treated as direct repetitions of the cycle-discrimination experiment.
A key alternative explanation is that simple graph features or exact cycle-decomposition algorithms suffice for
portions of the intervention task. The existing record does not contain a controlled same-task graph-algorithm
baseline or held-out biological structure benchmark. Accordingly, this manuscript makes neither a novelty claim
for graph cycle detection itself nor a claim of superiority to established spiking graph models or physical folding
predictors. Evidence here supports conditional computational sensitivity and reproducibility.
The binary classifier score and mask-disagreement fraction measure different events and should not be pooled.
Similarly, statistical variability over deterministic seeds is not the same as biological uncertainty over independent
specimens. The figure provenance, negative results and exact build/test records are retained to make those
distinctions reviewable.


6     Limitations and independent validation
The contact interventions in the original DNA/RNA studies were manually constructed, even though their
molecular accession records are real. The RNA energy model is not a complete nearest-neighbor thermodynamic
implementation, and the crossing-stem predictor is a deterministic heuristic candidate search. MMFF94
calculations involve short RNA fragments rather than full coordinate prediction of 76-nucleotide tRNA. No
ligand-binding measurements or held-out experimental RNA coordinate comparisons under the same evaluation
protocol appear in this record.

7     Reproducibility and availability
7.1    Data availability
All numerical observations discussed here are contained in the accompanying local research archive. The main files
are data/GRAPH_CLASSIFICATION_CONDITIONS.csv, data/ALL_CONDITIONS_FROM_RAW.csv, data/ALL_V9_V10_V11_PAIRED_
data/RNA_FOLDING_STRUCTURES.csv, data/ATOMIC_FORCE_RESULTS.csv, and data/RECORDED_TIMING_SECONDS.csv.
Each source-data lineage is preserved in the supplementary evidence package. No external DOI or public deposit
is claimed.


7.2    Code availability
The original executable research source and its historical archives are bundled in native_source_release/. The
paper-generation code is under publication_methods/. Re-execution requires a matching native development
toolchain and the documented scientific dependencies; the provided audit scripts can independently verify the
numerical records without rerunning the molecular engine. No GitHub repository update or new permanent
upstream deposit is implied.


7.3    Verification levels
Recorded output integrity and numerical recomputation are distinct from independent biological validity. The
portfolio verifier recalculates the 13,312 individual disagreement records and 208 grouped conditions and checks
100 graph classification rows. The previous software logs are historical and are labelled accordingly. A current
rendered-PDF build is evidence of document generation, not a fresh native molecular experiment.


8     Conclusion
The preserved native SNN records establish reproducible intervention-dependent responses for specified con-
structed molecular contact graphs and programmatically derived chemical, RNA and short-fragment structural
contacts. In the main graph-classification case, the strong cycle-breaking result and the non-significant ring-
preserving control together narrow the supported explanation to sensitivity to the tested graph organization.
Biological folding and ligand prediction performance, and relative superiority to established methods, require
prospective task-matched validation.
